What to automate first in a small business

Start with the work that is frequent, rule based and easy to get wrong.

Automation · 5 min read

Automation sounds like something for large companies. In reality, small teams benefit the most, because every hour saved goes straight back to selling, serving or resting. The trick is choosing the right first task.

A simple way to rank your options

List every repeated task in your week. For each one, score three things from 1 to 5: how often it happens, how many minutes it takes, and how often mistakes occur. Multiply the three scores. The highest totals are your best candidates.

Good first candidates

  • Invoicing and payment reminders. Invoices can be created from an order and sent automatically. Reminders can go out three days before and one day after the due date.
  • Order and inventory updates. If orders arrive by chat, web and phone, entering them by hand into a spreadsheet invites errors. Connecting channels to a single order sheet removes the retyping.
  • Weekly reports. A report that takes an hour to compile every Monday can be generated before you wake up.
  • Frequently asked questions. Opening hours, prices and delivery areas can be answered automatically, with a handoff to a person for anything unusual.

What to leave alone, for now

Do not automate a broken process. If the steps are unclear or change every week, fix the process first, otherwise you will simply make mistakes faster. Also avoid automating tasks where the personal touch is the product, such as thanking your best customers or resolving a complaint.

Rule of thumb. If you can write the task as a list of steps that never change, it can be automated. If it depends on judgment, support the person instead of replacing them.

Plan for the failure case

Every automation needs an owner and a way to notice when it breaks. Decide who checks it, what happens when an order does not sync, and how a customer can reach a person. A simple alert to your email is often enough.

Where AI fits

Forecasting is where AI earns its place in small businesses. With a year or more of sales history, a model can estimate next week's demand by product and day, which helps you buy and staff more accurately. Before that, clean data and simple automations deliver more value for less cost.
