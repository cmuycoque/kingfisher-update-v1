Reading your sales data without a data team

Four simple reports that tell you most of what you need to know.

Data · 6 min read

You do not need a data scientist to learn from your sales. Almost every point of sale system can export a spreadsheet. With a few filters and sorted columns, that file answers questions that would otherwise cost you money.

Start with clean data

Before any analysis, check the basics. Are product names spelled the same way every time? Are discounts recorded as separate lines or hidden in the price? Is every branch using the same item codes? Ten minutes fixing these saves hours of confusion later.

Report 1: Sales by product

Sort your items by total sales and by total gross profit. Rankings often differ. A product that sells the most may earn the least per unit. Look for a small group of items producing most of your profit, and protect their supply and quality. Look also at the bottom: items that rarely sell take shelf space, tie up cash and add complexity.

Report 2: Sales by day and hour

Group sales by weekday and time of day. This shows your real peaks and quiet periods. Use it to schedule staff, to plan promotions for slow hours and to decide when to prepare or restock.

Report 3: Repeat customers

If you collect phone numbers, emails or loyalty cards, count how many customers bought more than once in the last 90 days. A rising repeat rate means your product and service are working. A falling one is an early warning that appears long before total sales drop.

Report 4: Average basket size

Divide total sales by the number of transactions. Small improvements here compound quickly. Bundles, suggested add ons and minimum spend offers all push this number up without needing a single new customer.

Keep it to one page. Put these four reports on one sheet and review it weekly. The goal is a habit, not a perfect model.

When to go further

Once these reports are routine, you can compare branches, track the effect of promotions and forecast demand. That is the point where a connected dashboard and some automation pay for themselves. Until then, a clean spreadsheet is enough.
