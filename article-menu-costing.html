How to cost a menu so every dish earns its place

Know what each plate really costs before you decide what to charge.

Operations · 6 min read

Many food businesses set prices by looking at the neighbors. That is a start, but it hides which dishes make money and which only look busy. Recipe costing fixes this, and it takes less time than most owners expect.

Step 1: Write a standard recipe

List every ingredient and its exact quantity for one serving, including garnish, oil and sauces. If the recipe changes by who is cooking, the cost will too. A standard recipe gives you consistency for the customer and a number you can trust.

Step 2: Price each ingredient by usable cost

Take the purchase price and adjust for trimming and waste. If you pay 400 pesos per kilo of chicken and 20 percent is bones and trim, the usable cost is 500 pesos per kilo. Update these prices at least monthly, since supplier prices move.

Step 3: Compute the plate cost and food cost percentage

Add up the cost of every ingredient in the recipe to get the plate cost. Divide that by the selling price (without tax) to get the food cost percentage. Many casual restaurants aim for around 28 to 35 percent, though it depends on your concept and the rest of your costs.

Example. A pasta dish costs 68 pesos in ingredients and sells for 220 pesos. Food cost is 68 divided by 220, about 31 percent. Gross profit per plate is 152 pesos.

Step 4: Sort dishes into four groups

  • Stars: popular and high margin. Feature them.
  • Workhorses: popular but low margin. Adjust the price slightly, trim the portion, or pair with a high margin side.
  • Puzzles: high margin but slow sellers. Rename, reposition or train staff to recommend them.
  • Dogs: unpopular and low margin. Consider removing them.

Watch the hidden costs

Include packaging for takeaway and delivery commissions in your costing, otherwise your online orders may be losing money. Also track waste and staff meals, which are real costs that never appear on the menu.

Make it a routine

Review the numbers every month. When a key ingredient jumps in price, you will know straight away which dishes are affected and can respond with a price change, a substitute or a special.
